# Mortgage Domain Research

## Buy-to-let mortgages

A buy-to-let mortgage is a loan for a property that will be rented out.
The expected rent is an important part of assessing whether the
borrower can afford the mortgage.

The main parties involved are:

- **Applicant / borrower** — the person or company applying for the loan.
- **Landlord** — the owner renting out the property.
- **Lender** — the organisation offering the mortgage.
- **Funder** — the institution providing the money behind the mortgage.

Landbay offers buy-to-let mortgages and works with institutional
funding partners to finance them.

## Mortgage products

A mortgage product is a particular mortgage offer with its own
interest rate, fees and lending criteria.

Products can differ in terms of:

- Maximum loan-to-value ratio (LTV).
- Minimum interest coverage ratio (ICR).
- Minimum and maximum loan amounts.
- Accepted property and applicant types.
- Property location.
- Previous landlord experience.
- Interest rate, fixed-rate period and fees.

The mortgage term and the fixed-rate period are different.
For example, a mortgage might run for 25 years, while its interest
rate is fixed for only the first two years.

A tracker rate follows a reference rate plus an agreed margin.

## Principal, interest and monthly payments

The **principal**, also called capital, is the amount borrowed.
The **interest** is the cost of borrowing that money.

There are two main ways to repay a mortgage.

### Interest-only

The borrower pays the interest each month, but these payments
do not reduce the amount borrowed.

For example, on a £200,000 loan at 5% annual interest:

Monthly interest = £200,000 × 0.05 / 12 = £833.33

If no capital is repaid during the mortgage term, the borrower
still owes £200,000 at the end.

The borrower needs a plan to repay this amount, such as savings,
investments or selling a property, subject to the lender's criteria.

### Repayment

Each monthly payment covers both interest and part of the principal.
The outstanding debt gradually decreases until the loan is paid off.

For a £200,000 loan over 25 years, assuming 5% interest throughout
and no fees, the monthly payment would be approximately £1,169.18.

In the first month:

- £833.33 pays the interest.
- £335.85 reduces the principal.
- The remaining balance is approximately £199,664.15.

As the balance decreases, less interest is charged. If the rate
stays the same, more of each monthly payment goes towards the principal.

Interest-only versus repayment describes how the debt is repaid.
Fixed versus variable describes how the interest rate behaves.

## Loan-to-value ratio — LTV

LTV shows how much is being borrowed compared with the property's value.

LTV (%) = loan amount / property value × 100

For example:

- Loan amount: £225,000
- Property value: £300,000
- LTV: 75%

This would meet a product's maximum LTV of 75%, but not a maximum
of 70%.

Passing the LTV check does not mean the application is eligible
overall. The other product rules still need to be checked.

## Interest coverage ratio — ICR

ICR shows how well the expected rent covers the interest used
in the affordability assessment.

For a simplified calculation without fees added to the loan:

Monthly assessed interest = loan amount × annual assessment rate / 12

ICR (%) = monthly rental income / monthly assessed interest × 100

Annual rates need to be expressed as decimals in the calculation:
6% becomes 0.06.

For example:

- Loan amount: £200,000
- Annual assessment rate: 6%
- Monthly assessed interest: £1,000
- Monthly rental income: £1,400
- ICR: 140%

The rent covers 1.4 times the assessed interest. This passes
a minimum ICR of 125%, but fails a minimum of 145%.

The £400 difference is not necessarily profit. The landlord may
still have taxes, repairs, maintenance and periods without tenants.

ICR measures coverage of interest, rather than the full monthly
payment on a repayment mortgage.

## Stress rates

The **pay rate** is the actual interest rate charged on the mortgage.

The **stress rate** is the annual rate used to check affordability.
It may be higher than the pay rate to allow for possible increases
in interest rates.

This matters because a mortgage can continue for many years after
its initial fixed-rate period ends.

For example, a £200,000 interest-only loan costs approximately:

- £666.67 per month at 4%.
- £1,000 per month at 6%.

A rental income that comfortably covers the first amount might
not provide enough coverage for the second.

**Monthly stressed interest** is the monthly interest calculated
using the stress rate. It is a figure used for assessment, not
an additional charge to the borrower.

The stress rate is also not a prediction of what future rates will be.

### Who sets the stress rate?

The lender applies a stress-testing policy. Regulatory expectations
influence this policy where they apply, and funding partners may
also have their own requirements.

Public lending criteria do not tell us all the private conditions
agreed between Landbay and its funders.

### Examples from Landbay

Landbay publishes these general assessment rules:

- Two-year fixed and tracker products:
  the higher of 5.5% or the pay rate plus two percentage points.
- Five-year fixed products:
  assessment at the pay rate.
- Certain remortgage products:
  separate assessment rules.

For example, if the pay rate is 4%:

Stress rate = max(5.5%, 4% + 2 percentage points) = 6%

The applicable rule depends on the product and transaction.
The stress rate does not always have to be higher than the pay rate.

## Property types

### Standard property

For this exercise, a standard property can represent a single
dwelling rented as one unit.

### HMO — House in Multiple Occupation

Broadly, an HMO is accommodation occupied by people from multiple
households who share facilities such as a kitchen or bathroom.

The exact legal definition and licensing requirements depend
on the jurisdiction.

### MUFB — Multi-Unit Freehold Block

An MUFB contains multiple flats held under a single freehold title.

Different property types can have different lending criteria.

## Applicant type, experience and location

A product may accept individuals, companies or both.

Real lending policies can make further distinctions, such as
different company structures or the applicant's tax position.

Previous landlord experience can also affect eligibility.
However, being a first-time landlord does not automatically rule
out every specialist product. Landbay has dedicated first-time
landlord HMO and MUFB products.

Location matters too. Products may be restricted to particular
countries within the UK or have more specific postcode restrictions.

## Product eligibility and funder matching

These are two separate checks.

**Product eligibility** asks whether the application meets
the mortgage product's criteria.

**Funder matching** asks which institutions are willing to fund
that product for the application being assessed.

Funders have different risk appetites. For example, one might
support only standard properties, while another accepts HMOs
but requires a lower LTV.

An application could therefore have:

- A product that is not eligible.
- An eligible product with one or more matching funders.
- An eligible product with no matching funders.

An eligible product should not disappear from the result just
because no funder matches. The response should make that distinction
clear and explain why funding is unavailable.

A bank's own mortgage products do not necessarily use the same
criteria as its funding arrangements with Landbay.

## What this means for the application

- Keep product eligibility separate from funder matching.
- Return clear reasons when a rule is not met.
- Distinguish invalid input from a valid application that fails
  lending criteria.
- Keep thresholds and supported categories explicit.
- Record the chosen simplifications in assumptions.md.
- Clearly label sample products and funders as fictional.
